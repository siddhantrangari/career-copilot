Forward Deployed Engineer training Customer-focused solutions: FDE, Solutions, Demo and Value Engineering roles

1 · What these interviews actually test

Not "can you code". They test whether a customer would trust you in the room, and whether you build the thing that moves their number.

  1. Discovery — you ask before you build. You find the metric that matters, who owns it and what it costs them today.
  2. Scoping — you turn a vague ask into a proof of concept in 2–4 weeks with a success criterion the customer signs off.
  3. Solution & demo — you explain it in their language and tie every feature to a pain they told you.
  4. Value — rough ROI from numbers you asked for. Never invented.
  5. Pushback & ownership — security, cost, "we tried AI and it failed", saying no, feeding gaps back to product, a deployment that broke on site.

So I bring deep build skills and real customer exposure, not a pure pre-sales background."

3 · Five frameworks to rehearse

Discovery (ask these first)
  • What happens today, step by step?
  • Which number gets worse when this goes wrong, and who is measured on it?
  • What have you already tried?
  • Where does the data live, and who can give me access?
  • What would make this a success in 30 days?
POC scope (one sentence)

"In N weeks we connect data source, automate one workflow for one team, and succeed if metric moves from X to Y. Out of scope: …"

Demo storyline
  1. Their pain, in their words
  2. Today's process (the "before")
  3. Live: one workflow, end to end
  4. The number it changes
  5. The next step you want
Value maths (out loud)

volume × time saved × cost per hour, plus errors avoided × cost per error. Example: 4,000 invoices/month × 6 min saved ≈ 400 hours/month. Say "rough, from your numbers", and get them to confirm.

Objections — A.C.E.C.

Acknowledge → Clarify ("what happened last time?") → Evidence (a real story or a small test) → Confirm ("does that address it?")

Saying no

"Not by Friday, and here's why. What I can do by Friday is X, which gets you most of the value. Y goes to the product team with your use case attached."
