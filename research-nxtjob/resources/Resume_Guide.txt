The Summary Section
The Summary section acts the same way as a fish hook on a fishing rod. The recruiter gets stuck like a fish and is forced to read ahead (and call you for the interview of course). The way to achieve this is in 2 parts-
      1. Relevance - show the recruiter you are exactly who they are looking for. Add the job title of the JD, include the required years of experience and add the key skills from the JD.
      2. Brilliance - blow the recruiter away by showing your biggest, baddest achievements from your previous or current role.  


Follow this formula - Accomplished {Job title} with {x years of experience} and expertise in {select 3-4 of the top skills from the JD}. Achieved {biggest, baddest, relevant achievement} for {company}.




Part 1 - Show the relevance of your profile -> Accomplished {Job title} with {x years of experience} and expertise in {select 3-4 of the top skills from the JD}
  

Example - Two-time Founder with 9 years of experience and expertise in investment support, strategic initiatives, lead generation and operational excellence.

While hiring a resume writer is criminal, hiring a good career coach while switching jobs can be a magical move for you.


Hiring a resume writer can be misleading because it focuses on crafting a document that may not truly reflect your unique strengths and aspirations. It’s a one-time fix that often misses the deeper strategic needs of your job search.


In contrast, a good career coach is like having a personal guide through the entire job transition process. They offer tailored advice, help you identify your true career goals, refine your personal brand, and develop a strategy that aligns with your ambitions.

         * Goal Clarity and Alignment: They work with you to set clear career goals and create a tailored action plan, making sure every step of your job search is aligned with your long-term aspirations.
         * Holistic Support: Unlike a resume writer, a career coach provides ongoing support, including interview preparation, networking strategies, and personal branding, for a well-rounded job search approach.
         * Skill and Market Fit: They assist in assessing your current skill set against market demands, helping you position yourself effectively in your chosen niche and identify areas for growth or re-skilling.
         * Strategic Networking: A career coach helps you build and leverage professional connections, which is crucial for accessing opportunities in your targeted niche and gaining industry insights.
         * Confidence Building: Through personalised feedback and encouragement, a career coach boosts your confidence and presentation skills, essential for successfully navigating job interviews and negotiations.

The Experience Section
This is the most important section of your resume. Once the Summary and the Skills section have done their job, the recruiter and your eventual boss will be going through this section to evaluate the depth of your knowledge and skills and then decide whether you deserve an interview call or not.


The key aspect here is to showcase your application of specific skills and the impact that you achieved for the organisation.
Understanding your Impact
Impact can be of two types - 
         1. Business Impact (saved money/time, earned revenue for the company, etc)
         2. Technical Impact (which means you did the job really well) - this depends on the specific role.   


A great technique to uncover the impact you’ve created for your organisation is to use the Before-After sales technique. (Remember, you are selling yourself!)


Do you remember the loud TV ads for Chinese products in the late 90s and early 2000s. A particularly funny one was a product called the Yoko Height Increaser - which was basically a shoe-sole with pointy tips to stimulate your feet (and apparently your height). 


The script went like this - Earlier my height was just 5’7” and I was very under confident. No girl would talk to me and I’m unable to get married. But after using Yoko Height Increaser, I grew 4” and now I’m 5’11”. Suddenly all the girls are noticing me and there are several replies on my Jeevansaathi profile. I am very happy, my life has completely changed. 😛


A powerful sales technique is to highlight the painful before-situation and the rosy after-effects of a product. Can you apply this to your work? When you joined your company, or you were assigned to a project, what were the painful parts? What was broken? What was everyone complaining about? And after you spent months fixing it, what were the things that your manager praised you for? What were the positives on your Performance Appraisal?




If we were to apply this technique to the technical impact statements we used earlier, these are the challenges (before-situation) that each professional likely encountered and solved before achieving the results (after-situation):

The Education Section
This section usually serves as a check-point to whether you have the necessary educational qualifications required for the role. Look out for specific requirements such as MBA or certain certifications in the JD and make sure you add them here. 


This section is the easiest to build, but just avoid these mistakes-


            * This section is always at the end. To highlight really important certs/degrees, use the title in the Personal Details section.
            * Don’t include your 12th and 10th marks. School time is over.
            * Write your degree in full as well as the short-hand to accommodate for all ATS requirements. Example - Bachelor of Technology (B.Tech), Masters in Business Administration (MBA), etc
            * Don’t write your GPA (unless it’s really worth showing off)
            * Add those certifications that are in-demand in the industry. Six Sigma, SAP or PMP is great. Coursera or Udemy is terrible. Nobody cares about these.
